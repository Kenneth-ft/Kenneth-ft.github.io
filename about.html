About Me

The intersection of mechanical intuition, hands-on diagnostics, and deterministic embedded software.

Engineering from the Ground Up

I am a Mechatronic Systems Engineering student at Simon Fraser University (SFU) with an uncommon path into embedded systems. Before writing bare-metal C register drivers or routing high-speed SPI buses on PCBs, I spent four years working as a bicycle mechanic and clinic instructor at Mountain Equipment Co-op (MEC).

In a busy bike shop, there is no room for vague theories. When a customer brings in a bike with mysterious shifting hesitation, hydraulic brake sponge, or bearing play, you must listen, feel, isolate variables, and diagnose the physical root cause while the customer is standing in front of you. That environment forged my disciplined problem-solving procedure: inspect thoroughly, understand the physical constraints first, and build reliable solutions that endure real-world vibration, contamination, and stress.

Bridging the Physical and Digital

When I transitioned from Capilano University to SFU Mechatronics, I realized that modern engineering is frequently fractured: software engineers often treat hardware as an idealized black box, while mechanical engineers can treat electronics as an afterthought.

My passion lies directly at that boundary:

  • Mechanical Design & Fabrication: Translating functional requirements into CAD models (SolidWorks, Fusion 360, Inventor), performing mechanical advantage calculations, and using lathes, bearing presses, and 3D printing to build physical assemblies.
  • Electronics & PCB Layout: Designing schematics and routing custom multi-layer boards in KiCAD and Altium Designer, hand-assembling surface-mount components, and verifying signal integrity with oscilloscopes and logic analyzers.
  • Bare-Metal Firmware: Developing deterministically timed C and C++ firmware on ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers (STM32), writing modular Hardware Abstraction Layers (HALs), and authoring custom device tree nodes in the Linux kernel.

Technical Leadership & Mentorship

Currently, as the Lead Systems Integrator for the Engineers Without Borders SFU Bionics Team, I coordinate across five specialized disciplines (mechanical, electrical, biomedical, firmware, and UI) to build an underactuated, tendon-driven 7DOF bionic prosthetic hand.

I also serve as an Electronics and Soldering Instructor at MakerLabs in Vancouver, where I teach adults and youth the foundations of Ohm's Law, circuit troubleshooting, breadboard prototyping, and proper THT and SMD soldering techniques. Teaching forces clarity; if you cannot explain circuit analysis simply, you don't understand it deeply enough.

Life Outside the Lab

When I'm not in front of an oscilloscope or a CAD terminal, I maintain clarity and focus through music and the outdoors. Whether playing piano, dialling in live church sound mixes, cycling Pacific Northwest trails, or crossing paths with the infamous SFU Academic Quadrangle raccoons between lectures, I believe hands-on creativity and real-world observation make for better engineering intuition.
